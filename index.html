One quiet number, painted.

Time in Daylight is one of the quietest measurements Apple Watch takes. It sits in the Health app, updated every day, and almost nobody looks at it.

It deserves better than a row in a list. Brightened reads that single number, averages it across the last seven days, and paints it as a gradient that brightens as light accumulates. You can tell how the week has gone from across the room, before you read a word.

When your light fell

A quiet band shows when your daylight came, morning on the left and evening on the right. It describes your day. It does not grade it.

A goal that is yours

Set a daily goal that fits the life you actually have, and change it whenever it stops fitting. Brightened uses it to shade the gradient and nothing else.

No streaks

No badges, no notifications asking where you have been, no red. A quiet week is just a quiet week, and the app says nothing about it.

Entirely on your devices

One measurement read from Apple Health, and nothing else. No accounts, no servers, no tracking. Your goal travels from your phone to your watch over their own connection.

What you need

Time in Daylight is recorded by Apple Watch, Series 6 and later, and Brightened reads it from Apple Health. Without a paired Apple Watch there is no daylight data to show, and the app says so plainly rather than pretending otherwise. The Apple Watch app requires watchOS 27. On iPad, Brightened shows the same daylight once your Health data syncs to it through iCloud.
